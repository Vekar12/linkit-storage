Product & Supply Chain — Entry-Level Role Reference

What companies like Uber, P&G, Amazon, Unilever expect at entry level. Use this to frame ICICI resume pointers in their language.

Typical Job Titles

Company Titles
Uber Operations & Logistics Associate, APM, Marketplace Ops, Strategy & Planning Associate
P&G Assistant Brand Manager, Supply Chain Analyst, Product Supply Analyst, Demand Planner
Amazon Area Manager, Product Manager (L4/L5), Supply Chain Analyst, Program Manager
Unilever Supply Chain Management Trainee (UFLP), Assistant Brand Manager, Demand Planner
Flipkart Supply Chain Analyst, Category Ops Associate, Business Analyst

What the Day-to-Day Looks Like

Product Management

  • Define requirements from user insights + data → write PRDs
  • Prioritize backlog (RICE, ICE, MoSCoW)
  • Ship features with engineering & design
  • Run A/B tests, analyze, iterate
  • Track KPIs: adoption, retention, NPS

Operations & Logistics

  • Manage workflows, ensure SLA adherence
  • Identify bottlenecks from operational data
  • Build/improve SOPs
  • Launch new markets or product lines in a region
  • Root cause analysis on failures

Supply Chain

  • Demand forecasting & inventory planning
  • Optimize logistics cost & lead times
  • Vendor/supplier management & scorecards
  • Drive S&OP process
  • Continuous improvement (Lean, Six Sigma)

Top 10 Themes Across All JDs

  1. Data-driven decision making (SQL, Excel, dashboards)
  2. Cross-functional collaboration & stakeholder management
  3. Process optimization & operational efficiency
  4. Ownership mentality & bias for action
  5. Customer obsession
  6. Structured problem-solving under ambiguity
  7. Quantified impact & results orientation
  8. Scalability thinking — building for growth
  9. Communication — translating analysis into action
  10. Continuous improvement (Lean / Kaizen / experimentation)

Skills They Look For

Hard Skills

  • Data analysis / SQL
  • Advanced Excel / Google Sheets
  • Python / R (nice-to-have for FMCG, expected in tech)
  • Tableau / Power BI / Looker
  • A/B testing / experimentation
  • Forecasting / demand planning
  • ERP systems (SAP, Oracle) — for SC roles

Soft Skills

  • Stakeholder management across functions
  • Structured problem-solving
  • Influence without authority
  • Comfort with ambiguity
  • Analytical rigor

Resume Bullet Formula

Action Verb + What You Did + How/Using What + Quantified Impact

Strong Action Verbs

  • Operations/SC: Streamlined, Optimized, Automated, Reduced, Orchestrated, Scaled, Redesigned
  • Product: Launched, Drove, Defined, Shipped, Iterated, Validated, Analyzed
  • Brand: Developed, Executed, Grew, Repositioned, Led, Managed (P&L)

Example Bullets (Gold Standard)

Operations / Supply Chain

  • "Streamlined warehouse picking process by redesigning layout and implementing batch-picking, reducing order fulfillment time by 23%"
  • "Led vendor performance review covering 15+ suppliers; improved on-time delivery from 82% to 94% in 6 months"
  • "Reduced last-mile delivery cost by 18% by optimizing route clustering for 3 delivery hubs"

Product Management

  • "Shipped in-app feature improving onboarding completion rate by 15%, impacting 10K+ new users/month"
  • "Reduced customer support tickets by 20% by identifying UX friction points through funnel analysis"
  • "Prioritized backlog of 40+ feature requests using RICE framework; aligned engineering on quarterly roadmap"

What Recruiters Scan For

  1. Quantified impact — %, INR/USD, volume, time saved, scale
  2. Scope indicators — team size, budget, geography, stakeholder count
  3. Tools woven into accomplishments (not listed in isolation)
  4. Business language — P&L, conversion rate, NPS, OTIF, TAT
  5. Ownership signals — "Led", "Owned", "Drove" over "Assisted", "Helped"
  6. Cross-functional work — beyond your silo

What to Avoid

  • "Responsible for..." (listing duties, not achievements)
  • Vague bullets with no numbers
  • Over-indexing on tools without business context
  • Internal jargon that outsiders won't understand
